So we have been working for about eighteen months now on extending the school connectivity map to health facilities and the first thing to say is that it was much harder than we thought because the data that exists for health is nothing like the data that exists for schools, ministries of health keep their facility registries in all sorts of formats and often the location is a district name and not a coordinate, and in the Gambia which was our first pilot we spent almost four months just cleaning the registry with the ministry before a single dot could go on the map, after that Zambia went faster because we reused the cleaning scripts and the ministry there already had a geolocated master facility list, and Sierra Leone is in progress right now with about sixty percent of facilities located. What we learned is that the map itself is the easy part, the hard part is the agreement with the ministry about what counts as an official facility and who is allowed to correct the data, and that the open datasets like healthsites are useful as a starting point but nobody in a ministry will act on them until they have been verified, which is actually the argument for investing in official data in the first place. The next step is real time telemetry from the facilities that have a connection, we have a pilot design for three countries, and in parallel the product team is looking at how the map should work when it is not only schools and health but any kind of facility, because emergency services and refugee settlements have already asked. I need a deck for the steering committee that tells this story honestly, including the delays, and ends with the three decisions we need from them: approve the telemetry pilot, fund the data verification work in two more countries, and agree the governance model with the ministries.
